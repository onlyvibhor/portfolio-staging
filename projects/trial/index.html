Trial of the Century

An animal courtroom puts HR's most dreaded monthly question on trial.

A Panchatantra-style courtroom film that turned payslip confusion into a pop-culture moment for the HR community.

Reach the HR community, a relevant audience that is notoriously hard to engage.

RazorpayX Payroll needed awareness among HR professionals. They are the right audience, but a finance-heavy product is a tough sell to people who rarely engage with branded content.

A recent launch gave us a way in: an AI feature that lets employees talk directly to their payslips and ask salary questions. HRs showed genuine traction, which made us dig deeper into why.

For employees, Salary Day is the most anticipated day of the month. For HR, it is a week-long nightmare.

The same questions, every month, and everyone had accepted it.

The moment salaries hit accounts, inboxes flood: Why did my salary change? What is this deduction? Not out of carelessness, but because payslips are dense with abbreviations, tax codes and compliance jargon that few people understand.

Over time the dependency became normal. HRs absorbed it, employees assumed it. That cultural resignation became our creative brief.

If the same questions come back every month, the system needs fixing, not the people.

We reframed repeated salary queries as a payroll design flaw and put the repetition on trial, in an animal courtroom inspired by the Panchatantra and an unmistakable piece of Hindi cinema, the sawaal pe sawaal courtroom scene from Damini.

- Porcumar Rao, a porcupine, takes every recurring query like a spoke in his back. - Chulbull Pandey, the aggressive bull, channels the courtroom cross-examination. - Hathiram Trankalp, a wise elephant, presides over the chaos. - The employees appear as a flock of chirping birds.

Within the story, RazorpayX Payroll introduces R.A.Y., an AI assistant that reads the payslip and explains any change instantly, in simple language, without HR stepping in.

Animation-quality world-building without a single traditional animation tool.

  1. Explore. Character and mood explorations, iterated until the tone and personalities felt right.
  2. Lock the world. A digital map of the courtroom with every camera position fixed before a frame was generated: spatial logic instead of prompt randomness.
  3. Frame. Camera and framing prompts written with ChatGPT and Gemini, then executed through Nano Banana for consistent multi-angle shots, refined for continuity and texture.
  4. Animate. Performances generated and iterated until body language and facial expression felt natural.
  5. Voice. Professional voice actors chosen to embody each character, with lip-sync aligned to the recorded performance.
  6. Finish. Composited, edited and upscaled to 4K at 60fps.

The film

We gave HRs a place to be heard, and a soundtrack.

The Wall of Laughter was an anonymous digital board where HR professionals shared the funniest salary questions they had ever received. Anonymity mattered: HR professionals are cautious about being seen online, so humour became a safe way in.

"Inbox Avalanche", a debut music single, turned the monthly flood into a hook and earned 4.5 lakh+ impressions and 50+ saves. B2B content, competing with entertainment.

The movement spread through HR communities across India, including The Shape of Work, People Matters, SHRM India, Being HR and HR Breakfast Club.

  • 2.1M+ impressions
  • 135K+ total engagements at an 8.64% rate
  • 300K HR professionals reached, of a 650K online audience
  • 300+ brand mentions
  • 250+ reposts
  • 13% uplift in D&O leads

The narrative shifted from "HR failure" to "systemic gap".

What began as a feature launch turned into a pop-culture moment inside the HR ecosystem, with startups across the space (Plum, Spring Verify, ClearTax, Smartstaff, Jify, Fi) engaging with a campaign that, for once, was about HR.
